# Work around Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)

Compiled on 2026-10-18 by the literature_scout subagent from web searches. **How reliable it is:** abstracts were actually read for items 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 and the arXiv paper in item 15. Every other item comes from citations or search snippets, and the findings marked *unverified* must be checked before they are relied on. Nothing here feeds a number into this study's results.

## The paper

1. **Jegadeesh & Titman (1993).** [Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x). *Journal of Finance* 48(1), 65–91. NYSE/AMEX, 1965–1989. The 6/6 buy-minus-sell return is 0.95% a month (t 3.07; Table I, p. 70, checked against the PDF).

## Replications and extensions

2. **Jegadeesh & Titman (2001).** [Profitability of Momentum Strategies: An Evaluation of Alternative Explanations](https://doi.org/10.3386/w7159). *Journal of Finance* 56(2), 699–720. Momentum profits continued out of sample in the US over 1990–1998. Returns reversed 4–5 years after formation, but not at 2–3 years.
3. **Rouwenhorst (1998).** [International Momentum Strategies](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.95722). *Journal of Finance* 53(1), 267–284. Covers 12 European markets. About 1% a month according to papers that cite it (*unverified*).
4. **Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013).** [Value and Momentum Everywhere](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12021). *Journal of Finance* 68(3). Momentum premia appear in eight markets and asset classes, with a common factor structure, and momentum is negatively correlated with value.
5. **Daniel & Moskowitz (2016).** [Momentum Crashes](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2371227). *Journal of Financial Economics* (journal reference *unverified*). Momentum returns are negatively skewed, with crashes that are partly forecastable and come in rebounds after market falls. Relevant to the 2020 rebound in this study's sample.
6. **Jegadeesh & Titman (2023).** [Momentum: Evidence and Insights 30 Years Later](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927538X23002731). *Pacific-Basin Finance Journal*. A review by the original authors (snippet only).

## Critiques based on costs

7. **Lesmond, Schill & Zhou (2004).** [The Illusory Nature of Momentum Profits](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(03)00206-X). *Journal of Financial Economics*. Momentum strategies trade heavily in high-cost stocks, and the profits largely vanish after costs.
8. **Korajczyk & Sadka (2004).** [Are Momentum Profits Robust to Trading Costs?](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00656.x). *Journal of Finance* 59(3), 1039–1082. Estimates the fund size at which price impact eliminates momentum profits (*unverified*).

## India

9. **Sehgal & Balakrishnan (2002).** [Contrarian and Momentum Strategies in the Indian Capital Market](https://doi.org/10.1177/0256090920020103). *Vikalpa* 27(1), 13–19 (*findings unverified*).
10. **Ansari & Khan (2012).** [Momentum Anomaly: Evidence from India](https://doi.org/10.1108/03074351211193730). *Managerial Finance* 38(2), 206–223. Cited as finding momentum in India (*unverified*).
11. **Agarwalla, Jacob & Varma (2013).** [Four Factor Model in Indian Equities Market](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/four-factors-India-90s-onwards-IIM-WP-Version.pdf), IIMA working paper, with a [data library](https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/). According to the scout's reading of the library page, the 2025-12 release gives WML momentum about 10.65% a year (geometric) from 1994. This is a possible outside check for the Indian results, and a possible new data set.
12. **Misra & Mohapatra (2014).** [Evidence and Sources of Momentum Profits: A Study on Indian Stock Market](https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=26645) (*unverified*).
13. **Maheshwari & Dhankar (2017)**, [Momentum Anomaly: Evidence from the Indian Stock Market](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/jamrpp/jamr-11-2015-0081.html). **Garg & Varshney (2015)**, [Momentum Effect in Indian Stock Market: A Sectoral Study](https://doi.org/10.1177/0972150915569940): "robust" momentum over 2000–2013 (snippet).
14. **Singh, Walia, Panda & Gupta (2022).** [Risk-Managed Momentum: An Evidence from Indian Stock Market](https://doi.org/10.1177/23197145211023001). *FIIB Business Review* 11(3) (*unverified*).
15. **Recent NSE work.**
    - Pandhe (2026), [The Cost of Sector Constraints in Cross-Sectional Momentum: Evidence from the Nifty 500](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7376218), SSRN (snippet).
    - [Physical Momentum in the Indian Stock Market](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13245) (arXiv 2302.13245). NSE 500 over 2014–2021, using a physics-style momentum measure rather than JT's.
    - NSE's [NIFTY500 Momentum 50](https://www.niftyindices.com/indices/equity/strategy-indices/nifty500--momentum--50) and NIFTY200 Momentum 30 indices rank on volatility-adjusted 6- and 12-month returns. They are an investable comparison, but they are not JT deciles.

## What it means for this study

The literature broadly finds that 3–12 month momentum survives out of sample and in India. The open questions are costs (items 7–8) and crash risk (item 5). This study's sample runs from December 2019, so it contains the 2020 crash and rebound, exactly the kind of episode Daniel & Moskowitz flag. A short sample like this will give wide confidence intervals.
